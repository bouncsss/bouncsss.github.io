Boğaziçi University

Computational Social Science Society

We are students and researchers at Boğaziçi University who study social questions with computational methods. We meet weekly for hands-on sessions, and we build things together — models, datasets, and small simulations anyone can run.

What we do

Social questions, computational instruments

The society brings together people from political science, sociology, economics, history, and computer engineering. Our shared premise is that many social questions turn on processes we cannot observe directly — how opinions spread, how ties form, how what people say diverges from what they believe. Computational methods let us state those processes precisely and test them against data.

Sessions are informal and hands-on. Someone brings a problem or a paper, we work through the code together, and everyone leaves with something they can run. No prior programming experience is required to attend — several of our current members started with none.

Methods

Agent-based modeling

Building populations of interacting agents to see which macro patterns follow from which micro rules. We work mainly in NetLogo and Python.

Network analysis

Treating relationships as data: alliance networks, retweet networks, co-authorship. Descriptive statistics through to ERGMs and community detection.

Text as data

Turning documents, speeches, and social media into measurable quantities, with particular attention to Turkish-language NLP.

Machine learning

Prediction and classification applied to social data, alongside the harder question of when prediction is and is not the right tool.

Joining

Come to a session

We meet weekly during term at the Department of Political Science and International Relations. Meetings are open — no membership form, no prerequisite. Bring a laptop if you have one.

Write to us at the address on the contact page and we will add you to the mailing list for meeting times and readings.
